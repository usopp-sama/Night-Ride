# 02 — Story bible

All names and IDs here are canonical and repeated in `11-CONTENT-REGISTRY.md`. Chapter mechanics are in files 05 and 06; this file is what happens and why.

## 1. The world

### 1.1 Era and texture
An unspecified future, a few generations after the first permanent settlements. No year is ever shown. Dates are **Expedition Day N** for the player and **Hearth Year 34** for the settlement. Technology is sturdy and repairable: fusion-torch ships that take days per AU, pressure modules, rovers, paper forms alongside radio. There is no faster-than-light anything, no artificial gravity, no aliens, no AI characters.

About forty thousand people live off Earth, most of them in the inner system. Everyone off Earth knows what it costs to keep one room warm.

### 1.2 Hearth
- Hearth Crater, Mars. Founded 34 years ago by a landing party of 38.
- Population 340. Nineteen pressure modules, a grow campus, water and atmosphere works, a freight terminal, a remote fission reserve and a 216-array solar field (all as built in the prototype).
- **First ring:** the four original habitats around the commons. **Second ring:** the west residential quarter and its services. **Third Ring:** twenty-four homes for about ninety-six people, at the site the prototype calls "the third ring" (`expansion`, −65/−140). Its foundations were poured eleven years ago. The frames went up six years ago. It has never had power.
- Why not: the ring's life support needs a second set of six reactor cores in the remote power reserve. Solar cannot carry it through dust season.
- Who is waiting: twenty-four households on a list kept by Nia Okafor. Some are crowded families already at Hearth. Some are children born here who are now adults sharing bunks. Some are on Earth with their bags half-packed for a decade.

### 1.3 Cores
A **core** is a reactor core cartridge: a sealed cylinder 1.2 m tall, 0.4 m across, 180 kg, with a grey ceramic jacket, a copper-coloured lifting collar and a stamped proof mark. Six make a set. The game never explains the physics beyond: they are cast from rare metals found together in very few places, they must be proofed by a Compact transit port before any reactor will accept them, and a set lasts about forty years. Do not add technical detail.

### 1.4 The Compact
The Inner–Outer Settlements Compact is a treaty, not a government. It keeps three things: the **Registry** (who is owed what, in Earth orbit), the **transit ports** (where reactor materials are proofed; the nearest to the foundry is at Venus), and the **Patrol** (a handful of cutters that enforce the Charter). The **Mutual Aid Charter** lets the Patrol requisition any proofed reactor material in transit for a declared emergency. Article 9 seals the destination of a requisition so that nobody can speculate on another settlement's disaster.

The Compact's core queue is nine years long. Hearth is forty-first.

### 1.5 The Commons
The depot network (269 sites, as in the prototype) is a commons. Propellant is free to any ship. In return, custom says you leave a depot better than you found it. Inside Saturn's orbit depots are staffed or well kept. Beyond about 12 AU they are automatic, and over the years they have gone cold one by one. Only the anchorages at Uranus and Neptune still answer.

### 1.6 Vigil
Vigil Station, Triton, 30 AU from the Sun. 212 people: a deep-space observatory and relay that became a town. One reactor. Its core set is forty-four years old and failing. Vigil's request to the Compact for replacement cores was filed six years ago.

## 2. People

Line counts are budgets for the writer; see file 11 for IDs. "Tone" is the radio signature described in file 07.

### 2.1 The Courier (player)
Born at Hearth, twenty-something, never been farther than Phobos. Has never seen Earth. Flies the *Kestrel*, which belongs to the settlement. Never named, never shown without a helmet, never voiced. Other characters say "Courier", or a nickname: Mara says "kid" exactly three times in the game; Pip says "my friend".

The Courier speaks only through **replies**: at certain moments the player picks one of two or three short lines (≤ 8 words). Replies set tone and are remembered in later mail. Only three choices in the game change an outcome (section 6).

## 3. Premise and inciting situation

The Hearth council has stopped waiting for the queue. The Charter allows **self-supply**: a settlement may commission its own cores if it finds the ore, pays the foundry and has the set proofed. Nobody does this because nobody knows where the ore is. Mara has heard that a glaciologist on Europa is holding the last complete belt assay ever flown, and that she is the kind of person who trades favours.

The Courier is sent with the *Kestrel*, a crate of things people asked them to carry, and one instruction from Mara: *"Bring back six. Bring back yourself. In the other order."*

## 4. The plot

Days come from the reference trajectory in file 03 §4 and are approximate; the game never fails the player for being late.

### Prologue — Mars: "A little more daylight" (Day 0–1)
The existing repair job, extended. The Courier restores the east solar bank, visits the Third Ring with Jun, and meets Pip at the residential quarter, who hands over a list titled QUESTIONS. Tao gives a seedling tray. Bea gives a note for "whoever packed box 7-441". Eli asks for sky readings. Mara teaches docking in orbit over Hearth and says the instruction above. First postcard: home from the overlook.

*Purpose:* make the player love one place and count its windows.

### Chapter 1 — Europa: "What the ice keeps" (arrive Day 19)
Halden Station sits on the ice plain with Jupiter resting on the horizon, twenty-four Moons wide, never setting. Dr Aas has the belt assay. She will give it for the recovery of *Linnet*, her sounder probe, which went quiet under a pressure ridge. The Courier takes the ice crawler out between radiation windows and finds *Linnet* by listening: its beacon is too weak for instruments but audible through the ice. They winch it out. The assay names one deposit: the Occator vein, on Ceres.

Kofi asks the Courier to carry a recording of the sound under the ice to his mother on Titan. "No hurry. She's not going anywhere and neither, apparently, am I."

*Purpose:* wonder; the first long cruise; the first letter that arrives late.

### Chapter 2 — Ceres: "The weight of it" (arrive Day 35)
The Occator Cooperative already suspected the vein and could not afford to chase it. Their mass driver, which throws ore to orbit, is down. Imani puts it to a vote on the open channel: the Courier can have a consignment if they help haul it by hand. Twelve canisters, each a tonne, in 3% gravity, where nothing is heavy and everything is hard to stop. Arman returns Mara's torque driver with a thirty-year-old apology. Wen hands over a sample and asks, too casually, whether the foundry master could check her assay.

*Purpose:* fellowship; the player learns that favours travel.

### Chapter 3 — Mercury: "Lantern" (arrive Day 51)
The Lantern Foundry lies in the permanent shadow of Prokofiev crater near Mercury's north pole. Its furnace is lit by sunlight thrown down from mirrors on the rim. Yusuf will cast the set if the Courier works the mirrors: two of his keepers are out with eye strain. The Courier rides the rim tram, aims nine heliostats, and then stands at the pour and opens the gate six times. Six cores, each stamped with Yusuf's mark. Yusuf's crew retune the *Kestrel*'s drive as a thank-you (cruise tier II).

Yusuf: "I have made three hundred and twelve of these. I have never once seen one switched on."

*Purpose:* pride. The player has the cores. Act I ends on the high point of the game so far.

### Chapter 4 — Venus: "Proof" (arrive Day 57)
Cythera Port floats 52 km up in the cloud deck. Priya Nair proofs the set with real care; the stamp goes on each core. As the last stamp lands, the *Harrier* docks. Captain Varga reads Requisition Order 7 under the Mutual Aid Charter: all proofed cores in transit are required for a declared emergency, destination sealed. She is correct, brief and not unkind.

The Courier must release the six cradle latches themselves. Each one takes a held input. Each core is lifted out past the window. Varga hands over a receipt. The *Harrier* leaves. The whole chapter takes half an hour and nothing in it can be won.

Priya, after: she presses a blank proof certificate into the Courier's hand. "For the next ones. There will be next ones."

*Purpose:* loss without a villain. The player should be angry and have nobody to be angry at.

### Chapter 5 — Earth: "Position forty-one" (arrive Day 61)
The Courier does not land on Earth. The Meridian Registry is a ring station in high orbit, and Earth turns below its windows for the whole chapter, blue and enormous and unvisited.

Agnes Lindqvist hears the appeal and denies it in ninety seconds. Tomás Ibarra, who has stamped Hearth's requests for thirty years, takes the Courier to the stacks "to file the denial properly", and leaves them alone with the manifests. The Courier compares records and finds what Article 9 sealed: the cores were for Vigil Station, Triton, 212 people, reactor failing. They were loaded on the freighter *Marigold*. The *Marigold* stopped reporting nineteen days ago, at Titan. The Registry has noticed and has scheduled a review.

A letter from Mara is waiting. The council has voted. *"Eleven years of foundations can wait one more. Somebody out there is colder than we are. Go and see. — M."*

Nothing tells the player to go. There is no objective marker. They open the map and plot Titan themselves.

*Purpose:* the turn. The quest stops being about home.

### Chapter 6 — Titan: "Ligeia" (arrive Day 89)
An orange dusk that never ends. Ligeia Landing is a harbour on a methane sea. The *Marigold* is on the apron with a torn hull; its nine crew are alive. Captain Nwosu explains: wind shear on descent, cargo pod jettisoned to save the ship. Varga is here too, the *Harrier* grounded for want of a part, running a dredge that cannot reach.

The Courier takes a skiff out into the haze, following fog beacons, and finds the pod's recorder buoy. The sonar trace is plain: the pod is 160 m down in liquid methane, split. The cores are gone. The recorder also holds Vigil's last three messages. They are polite. They have stopped asking when.

Varga, alone on the quay, breaks her orders: the Compact kept a billet store at the old Verona Works on Miranda, mothballed forty years. Enough metal for a set, if anything there still runs. She cannot go. She gives the access code. Rosa Tindall gets Kofi's recording and listens to it twice. Titan's harbour crew fit a methane pre-cooler to the *Kestrel* (cruise tier III).

*Purpose:* the cores the player made are truly gone. What is left is a choice to make new ones for someone else.

### Chapter 7 — Miranda: "Verona" (arrive Day 116)
Nobody. A works at the foot of Verona Rupes, a cliff twenty kilometres high. Uranus hangs pale and sideways. The Courier wakes the place one system at a time: the pile, the heat loop, the crane, the arc furnace. Emeric Vance's caretaker logs play as each system comes up; he kept the works ready for thirty-four years for an order that never came, and is buried by the gate. The Courier pours six cores alone, with the skill Yusuf taught, and proofs them with Priya's certificate.

In the caretaker's room: a bundle of letters to a sister on Earth, never sent because the relay had gone cold. The Courier may take them or leave them.

*Purpose:* solitude and competence. The echo of Mercury with nobody watching.

### Chapter 8 — Triton: "Vigil" (arrive Day 148)
The Sun is a star. Vigil's lights are amber and few. Annika Sørensen meets the lander with a hand lamp. Davit takes the Courier down to the reactor hall, where the old set is running at a third. The Courier seats the six new cores, one at a time, the same six held inputs as Venus. With each one, a section of the station comes up: the nursery, the grow hall, the observatory, the long corridor, the commons, the homes.

As the Courier lifts the first core, their question goes to Hearth: *is this all right?* The answer is four hours and twenty-two minutes away in each direction. They act without it. It arrives about nine real minutes later, while the station's lights are still coming up, and Mara wrote it before she could know what was done: *whatever you decided, come home.*

The *Harrier* arrives two days behind, repaired. Vigil's council is recording what happened and asks the Courier to speak about Captain Varga. The player chooses what to say.

Mika, seven, gives the Courier a drawing for "the girl who asks the questions". Last postcard: Pip asked *"Can you still see us?"* The honest photograph is the Sun, with Mars somewhere in its glare.

*Purpose:* the peak. Giving.

### Chapter 9 — "The long way home" (Day 152 → 215)
31.6 AU, the longest leg in the game: ten and a half minutes of cruise in one unbroken stretch, on drop tanks that Vigil filled with every spare tonne it had. The hold shows six empty cradles. Letters arrive at their own pace from Ceres, from Mercury, from Venus, each a little evasive. The depots the player restarted on the way out flash their beacons as the *Kestrel* passes. One that they did not restart is lit too, with a note on its beacon. The route swings past the Sun; it grows from a star to a disc to a furnace and falls behind. Mars appears.

*Purpose:* emptiness that turns, without announcement, into peace.

### Chapter 10 — Homecoming: "The third ring" (Day 215)
Hearth at dusk. The *Occator Mule 3* is parked on the freight apron. Jun is waiting at the lander berth and says only: "We seated five. The sixth is yours."

Pip had written to everyone in the postcards. Imani put it to a vote. Yusuf cast a second set. Priya proofed it in a day. Tomás moved a file. Arman flew it.

The Courier drives across Hearth to the remote reserve, where the last core is waiting, seats it, and throws the switch. Then they drive back toward the ring as dusk becomes night, and the windows come on one at a time as they approach: eight that are always lit, and one for each thread the Courier tended. The classroom wall has nine photographs and, pinned beside them, copies of nine letters in a child's handwriting. Home 24 has a shelf.

Credits over the lit ring. Afterwards the game continues.

## 6. What varies

The plot does not branch. Three choices and sixteen threads change details of the ending.

### 6.1 The three choices

| ID | Where | Choice | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `choice_complaint` | Earth, with Tomás | **Sign** a formal complaint naming Captain Varga / **Withdraw** it | Signed: Varga has read it by Titan; her first exchange is formal, and thread `th_varga` can only complete through testimony "for". Withdrawn: she is unguarded at Titan. Tomás's later letter differs. |
| `choice_letters` | Miranda | **Carry** Emeric's letters / **Leave** them with him | Carried: they post at the next live relay; his sister's reply reaches the Courier at home, and thread `th_emeric_letters` completes. Left: a different, quieter final log entry; the thread stays open until a post-game return. |
| `choice_testimony` | Triton | **"She took them from us."** / **"She was trying to reach you."** / **Say nothing** | Against: Varga resigns her command; a short gracious letter. For: she asks for the Triton run permanently; `th_varga` completes. Silence: she keeps her post; the thread completes on the first post-game visit to any port where *Harrier* is docked. |

### 6.2 Threads and windows
See file 04 §5 and file 11 for the sixteen threads. The ring has 24 windows. Eight are always lit at homecoming. Each completed thread lights one more and puts one named object from another world in that home. An incomplete thread's window is dark with a card: *"Home 15 — waiting on fittings."* Dark windows light one per completed post-game journey, so nothing is lost forever and no player is told they failed.

### 6.3 What never varies
The cores are taken at Venus. The first set is lost at Titan. The Courier gives the second set to Vigil. The ring is built. Mara's answer says the same thing.

## 7. Themes, stated once for the writers and never in the game

1. **Rings.** Self, community, strangers. Each act widens by one.
2. **Taken / given.** The same gesture with opposite meaning.
3. **Delay.** Love arrives late and is still love. Every letter is already old.
4. **Maintenance as devotion.** Mara's torque driver, Emeric's thirty-four years, the depots, Davit's old cores. The heroes of this world keep things running.
5. **The stamp.** Trust between strangers is made of paperwork done properly.

## 8. Writing rules

- Radio line ≤ 28 words; letter ≤ 140 words; log entry ≤ 60 words; reply option ≤ 8 words.
- Each character has two verbal habits listed in file 11. Use them; do not add catchphrases.
- No character states the theme. If a line could be printed on a poster, cut it.
- No exposition by radio. Facts the player must know appear in the journal as one sentence.
- Every chapter has exactly one line that is allowed to be beautiful. It is marked ★ in files 05 and 06. Everything else is plain.
- Mara's letters are the spine: nine of them, written in full in file 11. Do not paraphrase them.
